01Where did it hit

Open water, debris, and the ground radar could not see.

Sentinel-1 radar sees through monsoon cloud. Lifeline compares same-orbit passes from before and after the flood, checks open water with a trained model, and marks steep slopes the radar could not see as not observed. Where the sky cleared, Sentinel-2 shows the same ground in colour: the grey river of debris is the flood.

03Who is cut off

Which villages can no longer drive to a hospital.

Lifeline routes every village to its nearest hospital or clinic on the road network before and after the flood. It repeats the routing many times over uncertain damage, so each village gets a probability of being cut off.

        How it works

        Four free data sources go in, and one ledger of facts comes out.

        Each step writes files into one run folder. The copilot and the report can only read the last one.

        1. Inputs

          Sentinel-1 radarBefore and after, same orbit
          Sentinel-2 opticalCloud-free scenes when they exist
          Copernicus DEMElevation, slope, height above drainage
          OpenStreetMapSnapshot from before the flood

        2. Where

          Radar changeOpen water, debris and scour
          Model checkSegmentation model checks the water map
          Not observedRadar shadow and layover masked

        3. What

          DamageHit probability per building, road and bridge

        4. Who

          AccessRouting to care, many times over uncertain damage
          ChokepointsRepairs ranked by villages reconnected

        5. Say

          Facts ledgerEvery number, with its source file
          Copilot and reportA guard rejects numbers not in the ledger

        Data rules

        • The pipeline never reads the official damage maps while it runs; a test fails the build if any reference file is read outside the scoring step. Settings were tuned on one official area and tested on the other three.
        • OpenStreetMap is a snapshot from before the flood (never later than 27 July 2026, the newest date the ohsome service holds), so mappers' post-event edits cannot leak in.
        • Every number shown to a user exists in the run's facts ledger, with the file it came from.
        • Pixels radar could not see stay unknown. They are never counted as dry.

        Limitations

        What Lifeline cannot do.

        It waits for the satellite

        Each Sentinel-1 orbit track repeats every 12 days; with three tracks over this valley a new image arrives every few days. The first after image of this flood came two days after it.

        It does not warn ahead

        Lifeline maps what already happened. It cannot predict a glacier collapse or a lake outburst.

        Steep slopes hide ground

        In narrow gorges radar shadow and layover hide parts of the valley floor. Those areas are marked not observed.

        Maps have gaps

        Remote trails, footbridges and small settlements are often missing from OpenStreetMap. Lifeline cannot route what is not mapped.

        Debris can look like bare ground

        Fresh debris on a dry gravel bar can scatter radar much like it did before. Some of it will be missed.

        Nepali needs a native reviewer

        The Nepali answers and report are machine-written. Until a native speaker reviews them, treat the English as the reference.
